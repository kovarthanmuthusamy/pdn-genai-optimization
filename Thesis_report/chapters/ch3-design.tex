\chapter{Design and Implementation}
\label{ch:design_implementation}

This chapter describes what was built and how it is configured. It covers the construction and
normalization of the multi-frequency dataset, the multi-modal variational autoencoder that acts as
the power-integrity surrogate, the latent-space optimization stage that searches for decoupling
capacitor placements, the experimental setup, and the active-learning loop that extends the training
set with newly simulated layouts. It describes the method and what is implemented; results are
reported in the following chapter and are not anticipated here.

Notation used throughout: a placement is a binary vector $\mathbf{b} \in \{0,1\}^{52}$ over the 52
decap slots of the board, the decap budget is $K = \sum_i b_i$, the power-integrity response is the
input impedance magnitude $Z(f)$ sampled on 231 frequency bins spanning 1--600~MHz, and
$Z_{\mathrm{target}}(f)$ is the target impedance mask against which a placement is judged. The
spatial power-integrity distribution at a single inspection frequency is a heatmap $H$ on the
$64 \times 64$ grid. \fillin{state the board under study (H-shape test vehicle) and its stackup /
port definition in one paragraph here; the AL config names the ECADSTAR design file
\texttt{H-shape.erf} with powerbus \texttt{Power\_GND} and port \texttt{IC1\_Port1}, but no committed
artifact in this repository documents the board geometry itself.}

\section{Data Preparation and Reprocessing}
\label{sec:data_prep_reprocessing}

\subsection{Raw Power-Integrity Simulation Outputs and File Structure}
\label{subsec:raw_pi_outputs}

Ground truth is produced by ECADSTAR power-integrity simulation. The raw export is converted into a
layout store by \texttt{pipelines/data/processing\_multifreq.py}, which follows the repository-wide
convention of a module-level \texttt{CONFIGURATION} block instead of command-line arguments. It
writes four artifacts: a \texttt{layouts/} tree, a \texttt{heatmap/} tree, a \texttt{PI\_freq/} tree,
and a \texttt{manifest.csv} index.

Per design, the layout store keeps the occupancy vector \texttt{occ.npy} of shape $(52,)$ and the
impedance spectrum \texttt{imp.npy} of shape $(231,)$. These two shapes are the fixed interface of
the whole framework: the occupancy graph encoder is built over
\texttt{NUM\_SLOTS = len(LABELS\_ORDERED)} slots and the impedance graph encoder over
\texttt{NUM\_BINS = 231} bins. A row of the manifest is therefore one (design, inspection frequency)
pair: the same layout appears once per anchor frequency, each time with its own spatial heatmap,
while occupancy and the full spectrum are properties of the layout alone.

Before statistics are fitted, the unbounded build applies a decap-budget filter: \texttt{MAX\_K = 30}
in \texttt{pipelines/normalize/build\_train\_norm\_unbounded.py}. $K$ is recovered per design from
$(\mathrm{occ} > 0.5).\mathrm{sum}()$ and layouts with $K > 30$ are dropped both from the statistics
fit and from the written output.

Three modalities are normalized separately.

\paragraph{Heatmaps.} The normalization mode is
\texttt{robust\_log1p\_per\_mhz\_unbounded} with \texttt{unbounded = true}. The forward transform is
\begin{equation}
z \;=\; \frac{\operatorname{log1p}(\Omega) - \mathrm{median}_{\mathrm{MHz}}}{\mathrm{IQR}_{\mathrm{MHz}}},
\qquad
\Omega \;=\; \mathrm{expm1}\!\left(z \cdot \mathrm{IQR}_{\mathrm{MHz}} + \mathrm{median}_{\mathrm{MHz}}\right),
\label{eq:heatmap_norm}
\end{equation}
where $\Omega$ is the physical impedance value of a heatmap pixel in ohms and the median and
interquartile range are fitted per anchor frequency. Two example entries: at 10.0~MHz the median is
0.05887173116207123 and the IQR is 0.03866304084658623; at 500.0~MHz the median is
1.3578120470046997 and the IQR is 0.6529430150985718. The per-anchor statistics therefore differ by
more than an order of magnitude across the anchor set. Global QC references are stored alongside: \texttt{clip\_min} $= -1.6791870594024658$,
\texttt{clip\_max} $= 6.275787830352783$, \texttt{z\_max} $= 11.23223876953125$ and
\texttt{background\_value} $= -3.1792$. In the unbounded mode these clip values are QC references
rather than training bounds. The exp059 config mirrors the same constants
(\texttt{background\_value} $=-3.1792$, \texttt{heatmap\_z\_clip\_min} $=-1.6791870594024658$,
\texttt{heatmap\_z\_clip\_max} $=6.275787830352783$) and sets \texttt{heatmap\_clip\_recon = false},
so reconstructions are not clipped to that band.

Foreground/background separation uses a physical threshold \texttt{BG\_OHM = 0.05} in
\texttt{pipelines/normalize/multifreq.py}. The robust clip percentiles 0.5 and 99.5 are defined in
the same script but apply only when the bounded mode is selected. Both behaviours are switched by
environment flags: \texttt{NORM\_ROBUST\_PER\_MHZ} (default \texttt{'1'}) and
\texttt{NORM\_UNBOUNDED\_Z} (default \texttt{'0'}), so the unbounded build that produced this dataset
sets the second flag explicitly.

\paragraph{Impedance spectra.} The 231-bin spectrum is normalized by a single global log z-score with
\texttt{log\_mean} $= -1.039238452911377$ and \texttt{log\_std} $= 1.7831873893737793$, fitted over
\texttt{layout\_count} $= 24979$; the resulting range is \texttt{z\_min} $= -2.7170498371124268$ to
\texttt{z\_max} $= 2.474900722503662$.

\paragraph{Inspection frequency.} The scalar PI\_freq conditioning value is normalized in
$\log_{10}$ space with \texttt{log10\_min} $= 6.0$ and
\texttt{log10\_range} $= 2.778151250383644$. The stored band edges are
\texttt{min\_hz} $= 1000000.0$ and \texttt{max\_hz} $= 600000000.0$, which is the 1--600~MHz span
quoted throughout this thesis.

\subsection{Data Splitting, Sampling, and Batch Construction}
\label{subsec:data_splitting_sampling}

The split is a layout-level holdout, not a row-level one: \texttt{split\_by\_design = true} with
\texttt{train\_split = 0.9}. \texttt{\_split\_indices\_by\_design} assigns every row belonging to a
\texttt{design\_id} --- that is, all of its anchor frequencies --- entirely to either train or
validation, and the assignment is deterministic given the seed and the sorted design keys via
\texttt{torch.randperm}. The dataloader's default split seed is 42.

The reason for this design is recorded in the code itself rather than reconstructed: the docstring
states that earlier code split frequencies within each design, which put the same layout in both
splits and ``leaked layout identity into val and is unsuitable for GP / AL residual evaluation''.
The experiment notes date the change to 2026-07-20, with seed 42 and \texttt{train\_split} 0.9
giving approximately 22481 train and 2498 validation layouts, and record that it required a fresh
retrain with \texttt{resume\_checkpoint} null, with prior artifacts archived under
\texttt{archive\_pre\_layout\_holdout\_*}. Those two counts are stated in \texttt{notes.md} with a
tilde and are not emitted by any artifact.

\section{Multi-modal Variational Autoencoder as a Generative Surrogate}
\label{sec:multimodal_vae_surrogate}

\subsection{Architectural Design}
\label{subsec:architectural_design}

The surrogate is a variational autoencoder \cite{kingma2013auto} with several input modalities fused
by a Product of Experts \cite{wu2018mvae}, conditioned on the decap budget $K$ and the inspection
frequency in the manner of a conditional VAE \cite{sohn2015conditional}. The exp059 class is
\texttt{MultiInputVAEPoeFreq}, a PoE-fusion subclass of \texttt{MultiInputVAE} that adds a PI\_freq
expert writing the private latent dimensions and removes the U-Net skip connections.

\paragraph{Latent structure.} The configured latent is \texttt{latent\_dim = 128} with
\texttt{heatmap\_private\_dim = 40}, \texttt{cond\_dim = 32} and \texttt{freq\_fourier\_features = 16}.
\texttt{imp\_peak\_dim} is not set in \texttt{config.yaml}; the constructor default of 8 applies. The
latent is partitioned as $z = [z_{\mathrm{shared}} \mid z_{\mathrm{peak}} \mid z_{\mathrm{spatial}}]$
with
\begin{equation}
d_{\mathrm{shared}} = d_{\mathrm{latent}} - d_{\mathrm{hm\text{-}priv}} - d_{\mathrm{imp\text{-}peak}},
\qquad
d_{\mathrm{layout}} = d_{\mathrm{shared}} + d_{\mathrm{imp\text{-}peak}} ,
\label{eq:latent_partition}
\end{equation}
which for the exp059 values gives a shared block of 80 dimensions --- as stated in
\texttt{notes.md}:15 --- and a layout block of 88. The decoder input widths follow the partition
(\texttt{vae\_multi\_input\_simple.py}:294--296, with \texttt{layout\_latent\_dim} defined at :174).
The heatmap decoder takes the full $z$ together with the $2\,\texttt{cond\_dim}$ conditioning vector.
The occupancy decoder takes only $z_{\mathrm{shared}}$ (80 dimensions) plus a \texttt{cond\_dim}
conditioning vector. The impedance decoder takes $z_{\mathrm{layout}} = [z_{\mathrm{shared}} \mid
z_{\mathrm{peak}}]$ (88 dimensions) plus a \texttt{cond\_dim} conditioning vector and a
64-dimensional occupancy graph context, annotated in the code as ``z\_layout + K + occ graph ctx''.
A placement is therefore forced through the shared block alone, spectrum-peak structure has a private
sub-block of its own, and frequency-specific spatial detail is carried in $z_{\mathrm{spatial}}$,
which only the heatmap decoder reads.

\paragraph{Product-of-Experts fusion.} Each modality contributes a Gaussian expert
$\mathcal{N}(\mu_m, \sigma_m^2)$ and the fused posterior takes the standard PoE form: the combined
precision is the sum of expert precisions and the combined mean is the precision-weighted mean of the
expert means \cite{wu2018mvae}. A unit-Gaussian prior expert ($\mu = 0$, $\log \sigma^2 = 0$) is
always appended as an anchor, so the product is well defined even when only one modality is present.
Expert log-variances are clamped to $[-6.0, 1.0]$ and the fused PoE log-variance to $[-4.0, 2.0]$.
\fillin{state why PoE was chosen over a mixture-of-experts fusion \cite{shi2019mmvae} for this
problem; the trade-off is discussed in \cite{kutuzova2021defense} but no repository artifact records
the decision for exp059.}

\paragraph{Modality encoders.} The occupancy expert is a graph encoder over the 52 decap slots laid
out on a $7 \times 8$ PCB grid, with 4-neighbour edges, optional self-loops and symmetric degree
normalization, following the graph-convolution formulation of \cite{kipf2016graph}; it is implemented
in plain PyTorch without \texttt{torch\_geometric}. The impedance expert is a chain-graph GNN over
the 231 spectrum bins --- edges between consecutive bins, self-loops, symmetric normalization ---
with a learned per-bin embedding, so neighbouring frequency bins exchange messages in the sense of
\cite{gilmer2017message}. Graph hyperparameters are not set in \texttt{config.yaml}; the applied
defaults are \texttt{graph\_hidden\_dim = 128}, \texttt{graph\_num\_layers = 3} and
\texttt{graph\_dropout = 0.1}. The 64-dimensional occupancy and impedance features are fused by a
\texttt{layout\_joint\_fusion} block (\texttt{Linear(128,64)} + \texttt{LayerNorm} +
\texttt{LeakyReLU(0.1)}) before the two latent heads. The heatmap expert is a convolutional encoder
with spatial bottleneck \texttt{HM\_BOTTLENECK = 8} ($8 \times 8$) and \texttt{HM\_DROPOUT = 0.05}.

\paragraph{Conditioning.} The budget $K$ is conditioned through a discrete embedding
\texttt{nn.Embedding(53, cond\_dim)} covering $K \in \{0,\dots,52\}$. The inspection frequency is
conditioned through a \texttt{FreqConditioner}: sinusoidal Fourier features over
\texttt{num\_fourier} bands are concatenated with the raw normalized frequency (input dimension
$1 + 2\,\texttt{num\_fourier}$) and passed through a two-layer SiLU MLP to \texttt{cond\_dim}. The
vector handed to the heatmap path is the concatenation $[k_{\mathrm{emb}}, f_{\mathrm{emb}}]$ of size
$2\,\texttt{cond\_dim}$. Note that \texttt{FiLM2d} and \texttt{FreqConditioner} are imported from
\texttt{experiments/exp043/codes/freq\_conditioning.py} (\texttt{vae\_multi\_input\_simple.py}:6), so
the exp059 model stack is not self-contained in the way the other experiment forks are.
\fillin{list every cross-experiment import in experiments/exp059\_capacity\_freq/codes/ (a grep for
\texttt{experiments.exp0} over that directory settles it); an earlier draft claimed the
freq\_conditioning import was the only one, which is not established.}

Frequency modulation of the decoder uses FiLM \cite{perez2018film}, applied as
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{FiLM}(x) \;=\; x \odot (1 + \gamma) + \beta ,
\label{eq:film}
\end{equation}
where $\gamma$ and $\beta$ are produced from the conditioning vector by a projection whose weight and
bias are zero-initialised except for $\mathrm{bias}[:C] = 1.0$, so the layer starts close to the
identity. \texttt{use\_heatmap\_film} is \texttt{true} in the config. \texttt{use\_multiscale\_film}
is \emph{not} present in \texttt{config.yaml} and defaults to \texttt{True} in
\texttt{vae\_model\_kwargs}, so multi-scale FiLM is active --- the value governing the headline
architectural change of exp059 comes from a code default rather than from the experiment config file.
With it enabled the heatmap decoder is FiLM-modulated at four scales: 128 channels at the
$8 \times 8$ bottleneck, 64 channels at $16 \times 16$, a 32-channel stage, and 16 channels at
$64 \times 64$.

\paragraph{Decoder path.} U-Net skip connections were removed; the code states the reason directly:
``No U-Net skips: decoder is latent+FiLM only (matches stage-2 / generation)''. The corresponding
modules (\texttt{OptionalSkipFuse}, \texttt{\_last\_heatmap\_skips}, \texttt{heatmap\_skips}) were
deleted, as recorded in \texttt{notes.md}:50--51. Spatial information about the placement instead
enters through an occupancy spatial tower, which scatters the 52-dimensional occupancy onto the
$7 \times 8$ grid and fuses occupancy features into the heatmap decoder at $16 \times 16$,
$32 \times 32$ and $64 \times 64$.

\paragraph{Layout-private head.} A layout-private head predicts the heatmap-private latent
dimensions from occupancy, impedance and the conditioning vector, and acts as the distillation
student for the full-encode teacher. The default hidden width is
\texttt{layout\_private\_hidden = 384}, a frequency FiLM is applied to the predicted mean, and the
output is squashed as $\mu_{\mathrm{priv}} \leftarrow 4.0 \tanh(\mu_{\mathrm{priv}} / 4.0)$.

\paragraph{Modality dropout.} During training a Bernoulli keep mask with parameter
$1 - p_{\mathrm{md}}$ is drawn over the three experts (heatmap, occupancy, impedance) and dropped
experts are replaced by the prior. At least one expert is always kept, and the heatmap expert is
force-kept once \texttt{modality\_dropout\_protect\_heatmap} is enabled. The schedule is
\texttt{modality\_dropout = 0.06}, \texttt{modality\_dropout\_start = 0.06},
\texttt{modality\_dropout\_anneal\_epochs = 18},
\texttt{modality\_dropout\_protect\_heatmap\_epoch = 25} and
\texttt{heatmap\_focus\_modality\_dropout = 0.03}. The design intent of the term is to keep the
encoder usable when experts are missing at inference time: occupancy is the only modality available
when a new placement is proposed and not yet simulated, and the active-learning loop scores such
placements through the occupancy-only encode path.
\fillin{cite an ablation or per-path evaluation showing that the occupancy-only encode path degrades
without modality dropout. No such artifact exists in the repository, so the enabling role of the term
is an untested design assumption rather than a measured effect.}

\paragraph{Discrete occupancy read-out.} Occupancy is decoded to a hard top-$K$ binary vector before
the heatmap decode, so the spatial prediction is conditioned on a CAD-realisable placement rather
than a soft one: \texttt{occupancy\_binary\_decode = true} with
\texttt{occupancy\_binary\_ste\_train = false}. The helper \texttt{topk\_occ\_binary} is fully
vectorised, using a descending-sort threshold to support per-row variable $K$ and avoiding Python
loops and GPU-to-CPU synchronisation on the decode hot path. It supports a straight-through mode in
which the forward value is the hard top-$K$ vector and the gradient flows through the soft occupancy
\cite{bengio2013straight}, but in exp059 training that mode is off.

\paragraph{Inference paths.} The model exposes several encode paths; the four that matter for this
work are \texttt{encode} (all modalities),
\texttt{encode\_cross\_modal} (heatmap, impedance or occupancy+impedance),
\texttt{encode\_layout\_latent\_full} (occupancy+impedance with the private head), and
\texttt{encode\_occupancy\_latent} (occupancy only). The last is the one the active-learning loop and
any forward design query depend on.

\subsection{Objective Functions and Physics-Informed Regularization}
\label{subsec:objective_physics_reg}

\paragraph{Reconstruction.} The reconstruction term is a weighted sum over the three modalities plus
an occupancy consistency term,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{recon}}
= w_{\mathrm{hm}} \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{hm}}
+ w_{\mathrm{occ}} \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{occ}}
+ w_{\mathrm{imp}} \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{imp}}
+ w_{\mathrm{occK}} \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{occK}} ,
\label{eq:recon}
\end{equation}
with \texttt{heatmap\_weight} $= 5.0$, \texttt{occupancy\_weight} $= 7.0$,
\texttt{impedance\_weight} $= 4.0$ and \texttt{occ\_k\_consistency\_weight} $= 2.0$. A heatmap-focus
phase starting at epoch 25 overrides these to heatmap 7.0, impedance 4.0, cross-frequency 1.1 and
dynamic-range 3.0.
\fillin{give the design rationale for the numeric loss weights (5.0 / 7.0 / 4.0 / 2.0 and the
focus-phase overrides). notes.md explains the capacity and FiLM changes of exp059 but not how these
weights were arrived at; a weight-sweep artifact would settle it.}

Occupancy reconstruction uses a focal binary cross-entropy with
\texttt{occupancy\_focal\_gamma} $= 1.5$ and a positive-class weight $(52-K)/K$ clamped to
$[0.5, 10.0]$, which compensates for the fact that a placement with small $K$ is a strongly
imbalanced binary target. The $K$-consistency term is a hinge that penalises
$\mathrm{relu}(0.5 - \ell)$ on target-on slots and $\mathrm{relu}(\ell + 0.5)$ on target-off slots,
where $\ell$ is the decoded occupancy logit.

The impedance loss is a composite over the 231 bins with weights
\texttt{impedance\_deriv\_weight} $= 1.4$, \texttt{impedance\_topk\_k} $= 20$,
\texttt{impedance\_topk\_weight} $= 4.5$, \texttt{impedance\_under\_penalty} $= 2.8$,
\texttt{impedance\_concavity\_weight} $= 2.5$, \texttt{impedance\_freq\_weight\_alpha} $= 2.0$,
\texttt{impedance\_dual\_topk\_weight} $= 0.75$, \texttt{impedance\_peak\_index\_weight} $= 1.5$,
\texttt{impedance\_peak\_mag\_weight} $= 2.0$ and \texttt{impedance\_num\_peaks} $= 8$. The peak terms
are ramped in rather than applied from the start: \texttt{impedance\_peak\_start\_epoch} $= 25$,
\texttt{impedance\_peak\_ramp\_epochs} $= 22$, \texttt{impedance\_peak\_focus\_epoch} $= 25$.

Heatmap reconstruction carries several shape-sensitive terms in addition to the pixel loss:
\texttt{heatmap\_pearson\_weight} $= 0.75$, \texttt{heatmap\_grad\_vector\_weight} $= 2.0$ and
\texttt{heatmap\_grad\_direction\_weight} $= 1.0$ with a minimum gradient magnitude of 0.08 and a
gradient Huber delta of 0.5. Extreme-percentile pixels are treated separately by top-region Huber
terms: \texttt{heatmap\_peak\_topregion\_weight} $= 2.5$ at $q = 0.95$ and
\texttt{heatmap\_valley\_topregion\_weight} $= 2.5$ at $q = 0.05$, both with Huber delta 0.5. A
soft-argmax peak-location loss uses \texttt{heatmap\_peak\_loc\_weight} $= 2.5$ at temperature 0.035;
the corresponding valley-location loss is disabled (\texttt{heatmap\_valley\_loc\_weight} $= 0.0$).

\paragraph{Latent terms.} The KL divergence is computed per dimension as
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{KL}_j = -\tfrac{1}{2}\left(1 + \log \sigma_j^2 - \mu_j^2 - \exp(\log \sigma_j^2)\right),
\label{eq:kl}
\end{equation}
clamped from below by \texttt{free\_bits} $= 0.12$ before averaging, so a dimension carrying less
than that much information is not pushed further toward the prior. Beta annealing is disabled
(\texttt{use\_beta\_annealing = false}) with \texttt{beta\_final} $= 0.03$, i.e. a fixed
$\beta$-weighted KL in the sense of \cite{higgins2017beta}. Three further latent regularizers are
added to the total: a hinge $w_{\mathrm{hinge}} \, \mathrm{relu}(|\mu| - \tau)^2$ with
\texttt{mu\_hinge\_weight} $= 0.1$ and \texttt{mu\_hinge\_threshold} $= 4.0$; a batch-mean bias term
$w_{\mathrm{bias}} \, \overline{\mu}^2$ with \texttt{mu\_bias\_weight} $= 0.05$; and a per-expert KL
averaged over experts with \texttt{per\_expert\_kl\_weight} $= 0.02$. A sigma regularizer keeps the
fused posterior standard deviation inside a band around \texttt{sigma\_reg\_target} $= 0.45$,
penalising values below the target and above $1.3 \times$ the target, with
\texttt{sigma\_reg\_weight} $= 1.5$.
\fillin{explain the choice of sigma\_reg\_target 0.45 and free\_bits 0.12; no artifact records how
they were selected.}

\paragraph{Cross-frequency, distillation and jitter.} Cross-frequency training decodes the latent at
an alternate MHz value and compares the result with the paired alternate-MHz heatmap supplied by the
pair lookup of Section~\ref{subsec:data_splitting_sampling}: \texttt{cross\_freq\_weight} $= 1.2$
from \texttt{cross\_freq\_start\_epoch} $= 20$, with
\texttt{cross\_freq\_layout\_mix\_prob} $= 0.45$ and \texttt{cross\_freq\_use\_encode\_z = true}.
Distillation transfers the full-encode teacher onto the layout student:
\texttt{latent\_distill\_weight} $= 2.5$ (private dimensions multiplied by 3.0, log-variance term
0.3) and \texttt{output\_distill\_weight} $= 2.5$, the latter implemented as a Huber loss between the
layout decode and the detached teacher decode. Frequency jitter is applied on the decode side with
\texttt{freq\_jitter\_prob} $= 0.6$ and \texttt{freq\_jitter\_log10\_sigma} $= 0.1$.

\paragraph{Spectral anti-blur term --- implemented but not active.} A 2D-FFT log-magnitude $L_1$
anti-blur loss (\texttt{\_heatmap\_spectral\_loss}) is implemented in
\texttt{train\_vae\_simple.py}:191--221, together with an optional Gaussian MHz bump that would
emphasise a mid-band frequency window. It is not applied in exp059. The weight key

\paragraph{Physics regularization --- implemented and configured, but not active in exp059.} The
module \texttt{physics\_loss.py} implements three physics terms. \emph{Radius influence} penalises
reconstructed foreground pixels that are cold where a frequency-conditioned critic says decap
coverage is weak. \emph{Critic supervision} supervises a \texttt{PhysicsCritic} CNN on the inverted
min--max normalised foreground of the target heatmap; the critic is a dual-branch network taking the
occupancy grid plus a frequency embedding (\texttt{freq\_emb\_dim} $= 16$) and producing a spatial
effectiveness map, and it is detached inside the radius-influence loss so that VAE gradients do not
corrupt it. \emph{Anti-resonance} encodes the requirement that every impedance peak be preceded in
frequency by a resonance dip:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
s^{\mathrm{peak}} &= \min\!\left(\mathrm{relu}(c - l), \; \mathrm{relu}(c - r)\right), \qquad
s^{\mathrm{dip}} = \min\!\left(\mathrm{relu}(l - c), \; \mathrm{relu}(r - c)\right), \\
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ar}} &= \mathrm{mean}\!\left(s^{\mathrm{peak}} \cdot \exp(-10.0 \cdot d^{\mathrm{prior}})\right),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:anti_resonance}
\end{equation}
where $c$, $l$ and $r$ are the centre, left and right bin values of the impedance channel and
$d^{\mathrm{prior}}$ is the running cumulative maximum of prior dip scores.

The exp059 config sets non-zero weights for all three: \texttt{physics\_ri\_weight} $= 1.0$,
\texttt{physics\_critic\_sup\_weight} $= 2.0$, \texttt{physics\_ar\_weight} $= 0.5$, with
\texttt{physics\_critic\_warmup\_epochs} $= 11$, \texttt{physics\_slope\_anneal\_epochs} $= 22$ and
\texttt{physics\_fg\_clip\_min} $= -1.6791870594024658$. \texttt{PhysicsLoss} is instantiated at
\texttt{train\_core.py}:1393, and its parameters do reach the optimizer and are stored in the

\section{Latent-Space Optimization of Decoupling Capacitor Placement}
\label{sec:latent_opt_decap}

\subsection{Problem Formulation}
\label{subsec:opt_problem_formulation}

Stage 2 inverts the surrogate. With all network weights frozen, the search variable is the latent
vector $z$ rather than the placement itself, following the latent-space inverse-design template of
\cite{gomezbombarelli2018automatic}. For a fixed budget $K$, the decoded occupancy probabilities
$p(z) \in [0,1]^{52}$ are read out to a placement by a hard top-$K$ selection,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{b}(z, K) = \mathrm{top}_K\!\left(p(z)\right) \in \{0,1\}^{52},
\qquad \sum_{i} b_i = K ,
\label{eq:topk_readout}
\end{equation}
and the predicted spectrum $\hat{Z}(f)$ over the 231 bins is compared with the target mask
$Z_{\mathrm{target}}(f)$. A candidate is declared feasible only when the predicted curve lies at or
below the feasibility boundary at \emph{every} one of the 231 bins,
\begin{equation}
\hat{Z}(f_j) \le Z_{\mathrm{boundary}}(f_j) \quad \text{for all } j = 1,\dots,231,
\qquad
Z_{\mathrm{boundary}} = Z_{\mathrm{target}} - m,
\label{eq:feasibility_design}
\end{equation}
with \texttt{BOUNDARY\_MARGIN} $m = 0.1$. The objective mode is \texttt{'gap\_max'} and the loss is
computed in the normalized log space (\texttt{LOSS\_SPACE = 'log'}), so the base term rewards margin
below the boundary and penalises excursions above it,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{base}}
= -\,w_{\mathrm{gap}} \cdot \mathrm{gap}
+ w_{\mathrm{exceed}} \cdot \mathrm{exceed},
\qquad
w_{\mathrm{gap}} = 1.0, \; w_{\mathrm{exceed}} = 25.0 ,
\label{eq:stage2_base}
\end{equation}
where \texttt{exceed} is a mean of $\mathrm{relu}(\hat{Z} - Z_{\mathrm{boundary}})$ raised to
\texttt{EXCEED\_POWER} $= 2.0$ over the bins, plus a maximum-excursion term.
\fillin{write out the exact form of the maximum-excursion component of the exceed term and of the
gap term from optimize.py:313--329; the extraction recorded the weights and the mean component but
not the full expression.}

The target mask is loaded from \texttt{configs/target\_impedance.npy}. It is required to have shape
$(231,)$ after squeezing, is interpreted in ohms, and is converted into the model's normalized log
space by $(\log Z_{\mathrm{target}} - \mu_{\log}) / \sigma_{\log}$ when
\texttt{LOSS\_SPACE == 'log'}. The stored array has shape $(231, 1)$ and dtype float64, with minimum
0.8 and maximum 49.99999999999999.

\subsection{Optimization Procedure and Feasibility Assessment}
\label{subsec:opt_procedure_feasibility}

The search is a plain gradient descent on $z$ with everything else frozen. Settings are
\texttt{K\_LIST} $= 1 \dots 25$, \texttt{NUM\_CANDIDATE\_SEEDS} $= 32$, \texttt{NUM\_STEPS} $= 1200$,
\texttt{LR} $= 5 \times 10^{-2}$, \texttt{GRAD\_CLIP} $= 5.0$, \texttt{INIT\_SHARED\_TEMP} $= 0.8$
and \texttt{OPTIMIZE\_BATCH\_PER\_K = True}, so the 32 seeds for one budget are optimized as a batch.
Seeds may be drawn randomly per run and saved to \texttt{run\_config.json}, or fixed to
$0 \dots N-1$; the mode is selected by \texttt{LATENT\_OPT\_SEED\_MODE} and defaults to
\texttt{'random'}.
\fillin{reconcile the three different budget ranges in the framework: Stage 2 searches $K = 1
\dots 25$, active learning acquires $K = 3 \dots 10$, and the dataset is filtered to $K \le 30$.
Nothing in the repository explains the relationship.}

\paragraph{Straight-through top-$K$.} The read-out of Equation~\ref{eq:topk_readout} is not
differentiable, and this is the load-bearing detail of Stage 2. The implementation uses a
straight-through estimator \cite{bengio2013straight},
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{b} = \mathrm{top}_K^{\mathrm{hard}}(p) + p - \mathrm{sg}[p],
\label{eq:ste_design}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{sg}[\cdot]$ denotes the stop-gradient operator: the forward value is the hard top-$K$
vector and the gradient is passed to $p$ unchanged. The code documents the reason directly ---
``Without this the top-K argsort severs the gradient and $z$ never moves on the spectrum target.''
The alternative of relaxing the discrete selection with a Gumbel-softmax or concrete relaxation
\cite{jang2016gumbel, maddison2016concrete} is not used here.

One qualification matters for interpreting Stage 2. The STE is applied only on the surrogate branch:
when the impedance surrogate is loaded the forward pass is
$\mathrm{surrogate}(\mathrm{ste\_topk}(p, K))$, whereas without it the impedance comes from
\texttt{model.decode\_impedance(z, K)} and the occupancy top-$K$ is not in the gradient path at all.
\texttt{USE\_SURROGATE} defaults to \texttt{True}, so the STE branch is the default, but the fallback
VAE-head branch does not exercise it.

\paragraph{Regularization of the search.} Several terms keep the search inside the region where the
decoder is trustworthy and keep the 32 seeds from collapsing onto one another. A latent prior term
uses \texttt{Z\_L2\_WEIGHT} $= 0.05$ with \texttt{Z\_PRIOR\_MODE = 'agg\_posterior'}, penalising
$((z - \mu)/\sigma)^2$ against the aggregate posterior rather than against the unit prior, and a
posterior-boundary hinge with \texttt{POSTERIOR\_BOUNDARY\_WEIGHT} $= 10.0$ activates beyond a
Mahalanobis-style radius of \texttt{POSTERIOR\_SIGMA\_LIMIT} $= 2.5$. Spectrum-shape terms are
\texttt{SHAPE\_REG\_WEIGHT} $= 2.0$ with \texttt{SHAPE\_MIN\_STD\_DLOG} $= 0.12$, which penalises
degenerate flat spectra, and \texttt{SHAPE\_TRACK\_WEIGHT} $= 2.0$ for MSE tracking of the target.
Peak-alignment terms are \texttt{PEAK\_LOSS\_WEIGHT} $= 1.0$, \texttt{PEAK\_INDEX\_WEIGHT} $= 3.0$,
\texttt{PEAK\_MAG\_WEIGHT} $= 2.0$, \texttt{DUAL\_TOPK\_WEIGHT} $= 2.5$,
\texttt{IMPEDANCE\_NUM\_PEAKS} $= 8$ and \texttt{IMPEDANCE\_TOPK\_K} $= 20$, all overridable through
\texttt{LATENT\_OPT\_*} environment variables. Seed diversity is encouraged by penalising the mean
pairwise cosine similarity across the parallel seeds with \texttt{DIVERSITY\_WEIGHT} $= 0.35$, and
occupancy confidence by \texttt{OCC\_CONFIDENCE\_WEIGHT} $= 0.5$. Finally the anti-resonance physics
prior of Equation~\ref{eq:anti_resonance} is applied here with \texttt{PHYSICS\_AR\_WEIGHT} $= 0.5$,
overridable by the checkpoint config's \texttt{physics\_ar\_weight}. This is the physics term that is
genuinely active in an optimization objective, and it belongs to Stage 2 as implemented against
\texttt{exp038\_true\_multi}, not to exp059: as Section~\ref{subsec:objective_physics_reg} records,
no physics term enters the exp059 training objective at all.

\paragraph{Selection and reporting.} Among the feasible candidates for a budget $K$, the selection
metric is \texttt{SELECT\_METRIC = 'max\_ohm'}: candidates are ranked by the maximum predicted
impedance in ohms across the spectrum, and the lowest peak wins. The alternative \texttt{'mean'}
would rank by the mean instead. If no candidate for a budget is feasible, the run writes an explicit
\texttt{no\_solution.json} for that $K$ rather than emitting a best-effort placement, which keeps
infeasibility distinguishable from a poor solution in the downstream reports.

Surrogate feasibility is a screening verdict, not a sign-off. A placement that Stage 2 declares
feasible has only been checked against the surrogate's own prediction, and requires ground-truth
re-simulation in ECADSTAR before any claim about the physical board is made.

\section{Active Learning for scaling the model}
\label{sec:active_learning}

The surrogate is trained on a fixed pool of simulated layouts, and the layouts that matter for
inverse design --- placements the optimizer wants to propose --- are not necessarily well represented
in that pool. The active-learning loop closes this gap in the standard pool-based way
\cite{settles2009active, batchal2022batch}: generate a large pool of candidate layouts, score them,
send only the highest-scoring batch to the expensive ECADSTAR simulator, ingest the labels, and
fine-tune the surrogate on the enlarged set.

\paragraph{Entry point and control.} The entry point is \texttt{pipelines/active\_learning/run.py};
\texttt{active\_learning\_pi/al/pipeline.py} refuses to run directly and instructs the user to edit
the \texttt{CONFIG} block in \texttt{run.py}. The default config is
\texttt{active\_learning\_pi/config/exp059\_gp\_error.json}, with
\texttt{exp059.json} (Monte-Carlo acquisition) and \texttt{exp059\_random.json} (equal-budget random
control) named as alternatives in the same block. \texttt{COMMAND = 'full'} runs the whole cycle;
\texttt{PROPOSE\_ONLY = True} runs generation and inference only, with no ECAD simulation and no
fine-tune, which is the mode used for dry runs. The cycle is described here as eight steps ---
per-$K$ acquisition (generate, infer, select), PEB construction, ECADSTAR simulation, ingest with
normalization and pre-fine-tune evaluation, overlay construction with fine-tune, and post-fine-tune
evaluation with the decision report --- but the step count is computed at runtime and is 8 only when
fine-tuning and post-fine-tune evaluation are all enabled, otherwise 7 or 6.

\paragraph{Candidate pool.} For exp059 the pool spans \texttt{k\_min} $= 3$ to \texttt{k\_max} $= 10$
with \texttt{k\_sweep\_per\_pool = true}, \texttt{candidates\_per\_k} $= 1600$,
\texttt{worst\_per\_k} $= 80$ and \texttt{total\_ecad\_per\_cycle} $= 640$. Candidates are random
$K$-hot occupancy vectors over the 52 slots. Generation is seeded deterministically per iteration and
per budget: $\texttt{seed} = \texttt{candidate\_seed} + \texttt{iteration} \times 10000 + K$ with
\texttt{candidate\_seed} $= 42$, so a cycle can be reproduced exactly. Each candidate is also
assigned an inspection frequency from the grid
$[120, 150, 155, 170, 180, 200, 230, 250, 265, 270, 280, 300]$~MHz under per-$K$ strata quotas

\paragraph{Acquisition by a residual Gaussian process.} The acquisition mode for the primary exp059
run is \texttt{gp\_error}; \texttt{inference\_pool.predict\_candidates} dispatches between
\texttt{gp\_error}, \texttt{random} and \texttt{mc}, defaulting to \texttt{mc} when unset. The
\texttt{gp\_error} mode does not ask the VAE how confident it is. It fits a separate Gaussian process
whose regression target is the surrogate's own error on labelled data, and ranks candidates by that
predicted error. The configured settings are feature path \texttt{'pred'}, target \texttt{'p99\_ae'}
(the physical-foreground 99th-percentile absolute error), regressor \texttt{'svgp'},
$\kappa = 0.5$, \texttt{n\_fit} $= 4000$, \texttt{n\_inducing} $= 256$, \texttt{svgp\_epochs} $= 80$,
seed 0, \texttt{novelty\_weight} $= 0.0$ and \texttt{score\_mode} \texttt{'mu'}. Other residual
targets are available in the implementation: \texttt{peak\_px}, \texttt{peak\_soft\_px},
\texttt{peak\_sharp\_px}, \texttt{struct}, \texttt{mae}, \texttt{mse} and \texttt{imp\_mse}.

The acquisition function implemented is the upper confidence bound
\begin{equation}
a(x) = \mu_e(x) + \kappa \, \sigma_e(x),
\label{eq:gp_ucb}
\end{equation}
in the form of \cite{srinivas2010gp}, where $\mu_e$ and $\sigma_e$ are the posterior mean and
standard deviation of the error GP. With \texttt{score\_mode = 'mu'} the acquisition returns $\mu_e$
only, and the novelty term is added only under \texttt{score\_mode = 'ucb\_novelty'}. The production
exp059 run therefore ranks purely by predicted error and does not use $\sigma_e$ at all. The
docstring at \texttt{inference\_pool.py}:177--179 nevertheless states that \texttt{gp\_error}
candidates are ranked ``by UCB $\mu_e + \kappa\,\sigma_e$''.
\fillin{resolve conflict between inference\_pool.py:177--179 (docstring says UCB with sigma) and
exp059\_gp\_error.json score\_mode = 'mu' resolved at gp\_error\_surrogate.py:350--352 (mean only,
sigma unused); docs/gp-error-surrogate.md sec.5 states the mu default explicitly.}

The GP inputs come from a feature path that requires no simulation: the model encodes the candidate
occupancy with \texttt{encode\_occupancy\_latent}, decodes it, re-encodes the model's \emph{own}
predicted heatmap, and uses the resulting posterior mean as the GP input. This is what allows
candidates to be scored before any ECAD label exists. The fitted GP artifact is cleared at the start
of every per-$K$ acquisition (\texttt{per\_k\_acquire.py}:66--69), so a fresh fit is performed in each
cycle rather than a cached one reused.
\fillin{confirm the reason for clearing the cached GP fit each cycle --- the obvious candidate is
that fine-tuning shifts the latent space the GP is fitted in, but per\_k\_acquire.py records only the
clearing itself, with no comment or note stating why.}

This construction is assembled from three separate literatures rather than taken from one canonical
precedent. The sparse variational GP that makes it tractable at candidate-pool scale comes from the
inducing-point variational formulation of \cite{titsias2009variational} and its stochastic extension
\cite{hensman2013bigdata}, implemented here in GPyTorch \cite{gardner2018gpytorch}. The acquisition
rule is GP-UCB \cite{srinivas2010gp}. The underlying idea of modelling a separate discrepancy term
rather than trusting the primary model's own uncertainty is the model-discrepancy decomposition of
\cite{kennedy2001calibration}. No source found in the literature review combines them as a unit for
active learning of a neural surrogate in engineering design, and this chapter does not claim one.
Modelling error with a second model is also a deliberate departure from asking the network itself,
for example by Monte-Carlo dropout \cite{gal2016dropout}, which is the mechanism the \texttt{mc} mode
implements.
\fillin{state the design rationale for choosing the residual-GP over MC-dropout uncertainty in the
author's own words; the framework document motivates it but the decision is not recorded as a
comparison in any artifact.}

\paragraph{Controls.} The random control assigns uniform random scores from

comparison is equal-budget. The Monte-Carlo mode retains its parameters in the exp059 config:
\texttt{mc\_passes} 4, \texttt{mc\_latent\_passes} 4, \texttt{mc\_decoder\_passes} 8,
\texttt{mc\_decoder\_dropout} true, \texttt{score\_metric} \texttt{'auto\_bad'}, with score weights
\texttt{decoder\_dropout\_rce} 1.0, \texttt{latent\_heatmap\_mse} 1.0, \texttt{p99\_var} 1.0,
\texttt{p99\_range} 0.5, \texttt{peak\_loc\_spread} 0.5, \texttt{peak\_cv} 0.25 and
\texttt{prior\_tension} 0.35.

No comparative claim is made here about the three acquisition modes.
\texttt{docs/cursor-handoff.md} sec.3D still lists the exp059 acquisition A/B

relative merit to be established.
